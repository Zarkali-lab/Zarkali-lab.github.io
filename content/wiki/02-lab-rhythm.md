# Lab meetings

## Monday stand-up — 30 min

Fast coordination meeting. Each person covers: what moved last week, priority for this week, blockers, and any decision needed from the group.
Keep project deep-dives out of stand-up. Capture actions and move technical discussion to a smaller follow-up.

## Main lab meeting - 30-60min

All about the science. We will rotate to present data, review code, problem-solve, and practice conference presentations. 
If there are no fresh data each person will bring an abstract from a relevant paper they have read and we will discuss these. 

## Friday wrap-up — 30 min

Close the loop on the week. Share one useful result, one unresolved problem, and any work that should be visible before the following Monday.
This is also a good slot for very short demos, figures, pipeline changes or papers worth discussing.

## One-to-ones

We use one-to-ones for supervision, career development, project decisions and concerns that do not belong in a group meeting. 
Send figures or drafts beforehand when useful.
Everyone will have a one-to-one with Angelika every one or two weeks. 

## Other meetings
The **DRC Lunchtime meeting** is every Thursday at 12.30PM at 8-11 Queen Square with internal and external presentations. It is a great way to stay up to date with other research around the DRC and meet others in the department. It is a hybrid meeting but lab members are encouraged to attend in person. 

The **DRC-Hawkes journal club** is every Friday at 10AM and is a hybrid meeting at 8-11 seminar room and on Teams. It is great for staying up to date with neuroimaging methods and good way to meet others across both departments. 

The **FIL Project presentations** are given on Friday afternoons at 2PM for any new project that will require scanning at the department. They are a great way to learn about state-of-the-art techniques and see what kind of questions you might expect when the time comes for you to present your own project! Project presentations are hybrid in the 4th floor seminar room at the FIL and on Teams.

**Brain meetings** are given on Fridays after the project presentations, starting at 3.15PM. These are seminars given by world-renowned experts on a variety of different topics. Brain meetings are hybrid at the 4th floor seminar room and on Teams. All lab members are expected to regularly attend the Brain meetings and being there in person is a good way to make connections. 

There are plenty of other talks and events within a short walk of the department, at the ICN, MPC, Experimental Psychology and Birkbeck. Going to talks is valuable. You learn about new research and see good (and not so good) ways of presenting your own. But your time is limited, so focus on the talks most relevant to your work and protect enough time for your own research!

# Working hours

We expect everyone to be on site on Wednesdays (for lab meetings) and at least 1-2 other days per week. 
Being in the office during core UCL hours (Mon-Fri between 10am and 4pm) helps us learn from each other, and sparks ideas and creativity, so try to be physically in the office those hours as much as possible. However, a main advantage of academia is flexibility. If you are not testing a participant, as long as required meetings are attended and the work gets done, you can decide which hours work best for you and whether you prefer working in the office or from home. 

To ensure you have some down time when off, please make sure to mark periods of annual leave on the team's calendar and set up an out-of-office.
This will allow you to take a proper break!
Similarly, don't feel obliged to check emails or messages out of hours. Angelika may reply sometimes to emails at odd hours, due to traveling etc, but you should not feel compelled to do the same!

# Conferences
Attending conferences is a great way to stay up to date on the latest research and to connect with other researchers all over the world. You will generally be able to attend one or two conferences a year, depending on the stage of your project and the location of the conference. A general rule is that funding for conference attendance requires presenting work you have worked on within the lab, but this may differ for different people. 
Discuss with Angelika if there is a conference you’re interested in attending. Examples of conferences that are usually relevant to our work are:
- Organisation for Human Brain Mapping Conference https://www.humanbrainmapping.org/
- Alzheimer's Association International Conference https://aaic.alz.org/
- MDS International Congress https://www.mdscongress.org/Attend/Register
- International Lewy Body Disease Conference https://ic-dlb.com/
